CUSTOMER SERVICE EXCELLENCE ACADEMY
The Client Experience Playbook
Complete Two-Day Curriculum · Building Trust Through Every Interaction
The Mendoza Law Firm LLC  |  Facilitator Deck  |  Leadership Review Draft
What This Training Covers
This is your work
  • Verifying identity before any disclosure
  • Reviewing the file completely before answering
  • Answering what the record documents
  • Classifying and routing everything else
  • Setting an expectation the client can rely on
  • Documenting so a colleague can act on it
This is not your work
  • Preparing or completing forms
  • Reviewing or judging evidence
  • Deciding what a notice means for this client
  • Assessing eligibility, risk, or consequences
  • Predicting what an agency will do
  • Telling a client what they should do next
Knowing the line is not a limitation on your role. It is your role.
What You Will Be Able to Do
1
Apply the Standard
Define and demonstrate Professionalism, Accuracy, Compassion, Consistency, and Accountability as observable behavior.
2
Investigate completely
Complete the nine-step review and summarize only the facts relevant to the next action.
3
Classify correctly
Use the Four Questions to sort any request into a routine answer, handoff, callback, or escalation.
4
Hold the boundary
Distinguish the four information categories and deliver the boundary response without losing trust.
5
Route red flags
Name urgency, destination, minimum facts, client expectation, and required documentation.
6
Document and hand off
Write a note that answers all eight elements and a callback that meets all six standards.

A routine service issue is rarely routine to the client. Long timelines, family separation, work authorization, money already spent, and in many cases prior harm from a notario or a previous representative.
The Standard, Stated as Behavior
Professionalism
Approved verification completed before any disclosure. Client's name and preferred language used. No audible side conversation. Contact answered within the published window.
Accuracy
Only what the record documents is stated — and you can say where you found it. Nothing is guessed. An incorrect statement is corrected in the record and with the client.
Compassion
The concern is acknowledged before the facts are delivered. Plain language replaces jargon. Understanding is confirmed, not assumed.
Consistency
The same question receives the same answer regardless of who answers it. Approved language rather than personal phrasing.
Accountability
Every contact documented the same day. Every commitment has an owner and a date. The person who opened the loop closes it.

Where anxiety peaks
  • After filing, when nothing visibly happens for months — silence is read as neglect
  • When a notice arrives that the client cannot interpret
  • When a date is set: interview, biometrics, or hearing
  • When money has been paid for work the client cannot see
Same firm, same file — but the client is in a different emotional place at every one of these seven stops.
Practice Scenarios
1
Silence reads as neglect
A client has waited months and has heard nothing.
What is the underlying need — and what can you say that proves the file was actually reviewed?
2
Told the story before
A client has repeated the same account to multiple representatives.
What does the record owe them, and what does starting over cost us?
3
Wants certainty
A client wants a guarantee Customer Service cannot give.
What can you offer instead that is honest and still reassuring?

Every Request Is One of Four Things
1
Routine answer
The answer is a documented fact within your authority.
Verify, answer, set the expectation, document.
2
Operational handoff
Another team owns the action. No legal judgment required.
Route with the facts. Tell the client who owns it and by when.
3
Legal callback
Answering would mean applying law to this client's facts.
Build a callback that meets all six standards. Never preview the likely answer.
4
Urgent escalation
A red-flag family is present.
Follow the emergency or priority route now. Do not queue. Do not interpret.
Four Questions Before You Answer
1
Have I verified identity and authority to receive information?
2
Have I reviewed enough to understand the full request?
3
Is the answer verified and within Customer Service authority?
4
What action, owner, expectation, and documentation is expected?
If you cannot answer all four, you are not ready to speak.
Two Ways to Get This Wrong
Guessing

An unverified answer does not stay a guess. It becomes the client's plan — what they tell their family, what they book, what they stop doing.

The correction costs more trust than the delay ever would have.

Sending everything to the attorney

A callback queue full of routine questions is not caution. It dilutes the queue and consumes attorney time.

And it makes the genuinely urgent callback wait behind the routine ones.

Both failures look like they protect the client. Neither does.
Who Is Who
High-level only. Knowing which agency holds a case tells you where a question goes — not what the answer is.
1
USCIS
Processes petitions and applications. Issues receipts, biometrics notices, evidence requests, and decisions.
2
EOIR
The immigration courts. Hearings, court notices, and anything on a court calendar.
3
ICE
Enforcement, detention, and check-ins. Any contact here is an emergency red flag.
4
CBP
Ports of entry and the border. A client at an airport is an emergency red flag.
5
DOS / Consulates
Visa interviews and processing abroad. Consular matters run on their own clock.
6
Our internal teams
Which team owns which stage — so an operational handoff lands in the right place the first time.
Documents You Will See
Receipt notices
Confirm the filing was received and when. You may confirm receipt and date. You may not confirm what it predicts.
Biometrics notices
An appointment with a date, time, and location. You may confirm the logistics. You may not advise on attendance problems.
Interview notices
A scheduled interview. Confirm date and location; anything about preparation is legal.
RFEs and NOIDs
A request for more evidence, or notice of intent to deny, with a response deadline. Confirm that it exists and the deadline. Never state what to send.
Approvals and denials
A decision. Confirm that a decision was received. Never interpret its consequences — a denial is an immediate red flag.
Court notices
A hearing date from EOIR. Confirm the date and escalate. Missed or imminent dates are time-sensitive red flags.
The general meaning of a document is process information. What it means for this client is legal analysis.
Investigation
The Nine-Step Review
1
Identity and contact
Verified? Authorized? Contact information current under policy?
2
Client summary
Who is the client; which matters and assigned teams are documented?
3
Case type and stage
What process and stage are reflected?
4
Deadlines and notices
Any appointment, court date, interview, RFE, NOID, or response deadline?
5
Recent notes and contacts
What happened, what was promised, and is the request repeated?
6
Tasks and ownership
What is open, overdue, duplicated, unassigned, or already routed?
7
Documents and uploads
What is received, missing, or awaiting review?
8
Appointments and callbacks
What is scheduled or already requested?
9
Synthesis
What can be answered, what needs confirmation, and what closes the loop?
Where the Review Breaks Down
Stopping early
Stopping at the first note, or searching only for the answer the client wants. Complete all nine steps even when step 5 appears to resolve it.
"Uploaded" ≠ "reviewed"
Treating an uploaded document as reviewed or sufficient. Confirm receipt only. Never confirm sufficiency.
Missing what's open
Ignoring an existing callback, deadline, repeated contact, or conflicting note. Steps 4, 5, 6 and 8 exist to catch exactly this.
Narrating the file
Reading out everything rather than summarizing what bears on the next action. Extra detail increases confusion and risk.
The third one is the expensive one. A duplicate check is mandatory before creating anything new.
Safe Decisions and Escalation
Four Categories of Information
Factual case information
A current fact documented in the CRM.
→ Communicate after verification if within policy.
General process information
Approved, non-case-specific explanation of a step or notice.
→ Use approved content. Do not apply it to uncertain facts.
Controlled legal information
General law, form, or procedure description approved for limited use.
→ Use only as expressly authorized.
Legal advice and analysis
Applying law to facts, recommending action, assessing eligibility or risk, interpreting consequences, predicting outcome.
→ Attorney review required.
The first three are things you may say. The fourth is a question you carry, with the facts already gathered.
Six Questions That Stop You
Learn to hear these while the client is still speaking.
1
Am I eligible, disqualified, safe, or at risk?
2
What should I file, sign, say, disclose, or choose?
3
Should I travel, leave, return, attend, withdraw, appeal, or respond?
4
What will USCIS, EOIR, ICE, or a consulate do?
5
How will an arrest, entry, removal, marriage, divorce, abuse, or crime affect the case?
6
Can a deadline, denial, notice, or decision be challenged, ignored, extended, or cured?
None of these can be answered from the record. All of them can be carried.
The Boundary Response
1
Acknowledge
Show the concern was heard. "I understand why you want clarity before making that decision."
2
Set the boundary
Name the need for legal review. "That requires the legal team to review how the law applies to your circumstances."
3
State known facts
Share verified, safe information. "What I can confirm is that the file shows…"
4
Take action
Create the correct callback or escalation. "I will send the attorney your specific question and the relevant facts."
5
Set the expectation
Explain approved owner and timing. "I'll document today's contact and this will follow the callback process."
A structure, not a script. It always ends with an action — never a closed door.
Red Flags, Communication, and Mastery
Five Red-Flag Families
Government enforcement
ICE contact, detention, arrest, active law-enforcement contact. → Do not delay. Follow the emergency route.
Time-sensitive legal event
Court date, interview, RFE or NOID response, appeal, or other imminent deadline. → Verify date and source. Route under priority rules.
Safety and vulnerability
Domestic violence, threats, coercion, trafficking concerns, statements of self-harm. → Use the approved safety protocol. Avoid promises or probing.
Fraud, identity, money
Suspected impersonation, fraud, payment irregularity, document concern. → Protect information and route to the designated owner.
Adverse decision or change
Denial, revocation, termination, new arrest, travel or departure, family-status change. → Do not interpret consequences. Obtain attorney review.
Red Flags Nobody Announces
Listen for these
  • A third or fourth contact on the same unresolved issue
  • Mention of a lawsuit, the bar, or "someone else I talked to"
  • The client signed something, paid someone, or filed something themselves
  • Casual mention of travel, a departure date, a new job, or a job loss
  • A name appears that is not documented on the matter
  • A tone shift mid-contact, or sudden insistence on the attorney today
Do not do these
  • Do not interpret what it means for the case, even when asked directly
  • Do not reassure with a prediction — "I'm sure it will be fine" is a promise
  • Do not probe beyond the minimum facts the destination owner needs
  • Do not defer to tomorrow to avoid an awkward handoff
  • Do not leave the documentation until the end of the day
The quiet ones cost the most, because nothing about the call feels urgent while it is happening.

Trust, Documentation, and Handoffs
Frustration to Trust: Six Moves
1
Listen and clarify
Do not interrupt. Summarize both the issue and the impact. Ask focused questions.
2
Acknowledge
Name the understandable frustration without admitting unverified fault or promising an outcome.
3
Investigate and explain
Use the CRM and Dropbox. Say what is known and what is not.
4
Offer the next step
A realistic action, owner, and expectation. Never a vague "someone will call."
5
Set conduct boundaries
Stay calm. Use the approved warning and end-call procedure if behavior becomes abusive or unsafe.
6
Close and document
Confirm understanding and record the interaction objectively, the same day.
Most of the frustration is not about you — it is time, cost, silence, fear, and delay no one in this building controls.
Delivering Non-News
"There is no update" has to sound like evidence the file was reviewed — because it was.
Inflames
  • "Calm down."
  • "That's just our policy."
  • "There's nothing I can do."
  • "You should have…"
  • "No change, sorry."
Helps
  • "You're right that this has taken a long time."
  • "Here is exactly what I checked today."
  • "The last action on your file was on the 12th."
  • "Here is what happens next, and who owns it."
  • "I'll check again on Thursday either way."
Name what you checked, and when you will check again. That is the whole difference.
Eight Questions Every Note Must Answer
1
Identity / channel
Who contacted the firm?
2
Request
What exactly did the client ask or report?
3
Investigation
Which records, notes, tasks, notices, or documents were reviewed?
4
Verified information
What documented facts were communicated?
5
Action / route
What was completed, created, scheduled, handed off, or escalated?
6
Owner / urgency
Who owns the next action, and under what priority?
7
Expectation
What approved timeframe or next step was communicated?
8
Follow-up
What remains open, and how will the loop be closed?
The test: could a colleague who never spoke to this client act on your note?
Six Standards for Every Callback
Reason
One precise question or decision needed from the attorney.
Relevant facts
The verified facts needed to understand the question — and no unsupported legal conclusions.
Timing and risk
The actual deadline or red flag, and its source. Do not label every upset client urgent.
Client logistics
Best contact information, best time, and preferred language.
Action taken
What you already provided, what operational steps you completed, and the expectation you set.
Duplicate check
Confirm no active callback or escalation already owns this issue.
